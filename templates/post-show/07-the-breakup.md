# Post-Show: The Breakup Email

> **When to use:** You've sent 3-4 follow-ups with no response. The "breakup" email is famously effective because it leverages FOMO and simple human nature — it either gets a "sorry I've been swamped, let's talk" reply or cleanly closes the loop.
> **Best timing:** 3-4 weeks after the event, after 2-3 previous follow-ups
> **Expected response rate:** 10-20% (often higher than standard follow-ups)

## Subject Line Options

1. `Closing the loop on [Show Name]`
2. `Time to cross [Company] off my list?`
3. `Should I close your file?`

## Email Body

Hi [First Name],

I haven't heard back since [Show Name], so I'm guessing this isn't a priority for [Company] right now — or you're still buried in post-show catch-up.

Either way, I don't want to keep cluttering your inbox. If you're still open to exploring how we help with [Specific Problem], just reply to this email. Otherwise, I'll assume the timing isn't right and won't reach out again.

Thanks for your time at the show regardless.

Best,
[Your Name]

## Variables

| Variable | Description | Example |
|:---|:---|:---|
| [First Name] | Recipient's first name | Marcus |
| [Company] | Their company name | DataBridge Inc |
| [Show Name] | Trade show name | MEDICA 2026 |
| [Specific Problem] | The pain point you discussed | tracking exhibitor ROI |
| [Your Name] | Your full name | Alex Chen |

## Tips

- Keep this email very short — under 80 words is ideal
- Don't apologize for following up; be direct and respectful
- The phrase "won't reach out again" is the key trigger — it creates urgency without being pushy
- If they do reply, treat it as a warm lead, not a cold restart
- You can run a second version of this 3-4 months later if circumstances change
